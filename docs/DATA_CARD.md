# Data card

No marketplace simulator or persona corpus was provided with this offering of the assignment (the
Classroom starter kit was an example scaffold; the course staff confirmed students build their own
simulator). GigBid therefore combines **real public job data** with **clearly labelled synthetic
data**. Every record carries `"synthetic": true|false` and a `source` field.

## How the corpus defeats naive RAG (by design)

* The same project appears three ways: a case study naming the tools, a terse README ("Small internal
  tool. See main.py."), and a client review that never names a tool.
* Skills are implied rather than named ("schedule nightly data loads" means Airflow).
* Exact tool names matter (Celery, dbt, Pinecone) and real Kaggle posts are long and noisy.
* Past proposals mix good and generic writing, and their outcome matters more than wording.

## Gold labels (how they are made, and their limits)

Gold labels are produced by deterministic rules in the generator, **not by humans**:

* `relevant_evidence`: non-CV items sharing at least min(2, |requirements|) skills with the job's
  requirements. READMEs and reviews carry no skill tags, so they are never gold-relevant: a variant that
  surfaces a terse README is not rewarded for it.
* `action`: skip if scam; bid if skill coverage >= 0.6, the budget clears the floor rate and evidence
  exists; watch if coverage >= 0.4; else skip.
* `fair_band`: for real posts with a fixed budget, budget ±30%; otherwise a category/complexity rule
  anchored to market rates.

Limitations to keep in mind when reading results: labels share assumptions with the generator (skill
overlap), so retrieval metrics reward skill-tag matching; real posts' requirements are the skills
our vocabulary finds in their text; GigSim's client model is a hand-written function of hidden
preferences (plus an optional LLM for reply wording), so "reply rate" measures agreement with that
model, not with real clients. The hidden test set is generated by the instructor and never enters the
repo (`evals/datasets/hidden*` is git-ignored).

## Shipped retrieval design (R6)

Chosen by `evals/results/rag_main.json` (544 dev jobs with at least one gold evidence item, 3 personas),
confirmed by `evals/results/rag_ablation.json`. See the results table in the README; the decision
paragraph is in `docs/adr/0002-retrieval.md`.

## Privacy

Personas, clients and portfolios are synthetic and may be shown publicly. Real Kaggle posts are public
job ads; GigBid masks emails, phone numbers and payment details in logs and traces. No live-mode client
data or tokens are committed.
